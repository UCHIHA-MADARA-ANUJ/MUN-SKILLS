## 1. FINAL GSL

A machine that selects a human target has made a decision. International law has not decided whose.

Some will call this discussion premature. It is not. The first reported autonomous engagement is already on the United Nations record — and it remains unresolved. No one can say who controlled that weapon, because no law required anyone to.

Others say existing law is sufficient. The Secretary-General disagrees — accountability, he wrote, cannot be transferred to a machine. Then let the defenders of sufficiency name three things. The provision that guarantees a human selects. The standard for how much control is enough. The mechanism that assigns responsibility when both fail. **[PAUSE]** Twelve years. No names. "Sufficient" is not an argument — it is an assumption.

Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates — Morocco does not come to ban this technology. We operate advanced unmanned systems under human command — that is the point: this standard is operable. We propose three commitments. Meaningful human control, binding — because a safeguard that can be withdrawn is no safeguard. Weapons reviews against a shared standard — because self-assessment is not oversight. Recorded engagements — because what cannot be punished will be repeated.

So to every delegation ready to oppose three commitments this modest: bring a better answer. Who selects? Who answers? Until the law supplies those names, every arsenal runs on trust. **[PAUSE]** Morocco does not mistake trust for law. Neither should you.

## 4. FIVE LIKELY POIs + ANSWERS
1. **"Article 36 reviews and command responsibility already answer your three questions."** → "Name the standard, then. Article 36 binds only its parties — barely a dozen of 174 even run reviews, each to its own measure, and several major military powers never joined that instrument at all. A duty a handful performs in private is not sufficiency. It is the gap, described."
2. **"Isn't a binding push premature while the CCW is still working?"** → "Twelve years produced a characterization and principles, which we propose to use. Codifying what consensus built is not preemption of Geneva — it is punctuality. 'Premature' has been the answer every year; the technology has never once waited for it."
3. **"'Meaningful human control' has no agreed definition — you cannot bind the undefined."** → "The parameters exist: predictability, reliability, the ability to intervene and abort — responsible states already apply them nationally. Shared criteria turn practice into standard. 'Undefined' is the case for drafting, not for delay."
4. **"A convention without the major producers is a hollow norm."** → "A control standard asks no arsenal to shrink — it asks decisions to have owners. Norms shape conduct even beyond their signatories; landmines ceased to be respectable long before they became universal. The alternative to a rule some resist is a vacuum everyone exploits."
5. **"Morocco operates armed drones — this is positioning, not principle."** → "Under human command, within humanitarian law — which makes us the proof the standard is operable, not its exception. We discipline the decision, not the tool. And to any delegation reaching for regional allegations: allegations are not verdicts — if you want verdicts, you need our attribution regime more than we do."
*(Bonus POI, keep loaded: **"You endorsed the 2023 Political Declaration — which is it?"** → "Floors are built on, never hidden behind. The Declaration governs responsible use by humans. It is silent on the absence of a human decision. That silence is this agenda.")*
