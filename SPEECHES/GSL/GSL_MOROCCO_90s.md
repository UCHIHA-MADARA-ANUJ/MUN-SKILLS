## THE SPEECH

<!-- SPEECH-START -->
Even Washington's own directive on autonomous weapons requires **"appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force."** If restraint is doctrine at home, why is it **premature** as law?

Three states voted no on the last resolution — against talk itself: **Belarus. The DPRK. Russia.**

Delegations insist existing law suffices. Then three questions: **the rule that guarantees meaningful human control; the standard that defines how much control is enough; the mechanism that identifies who answers when that control fails.** [PAUSE] Twelve years. No names. "Sufficient" is not an answer — it is a **bet** that the question will never be asked.

Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates — Morocco does not oppose this technology; **we operate unmanned systems under human command**. Three commitments, already in our twenty-nine-sponsor text: **binding meaningful human control, because a safeguard that can be withdrawn is no safeguard; weapons reviews against shared criteria, because self-assessment is not oversight; recorded engagements, because accountability without a record is an assertion, not a mechanism.**

Most here will never build these weapons; all here can be struck by them. The majority did not create this gap. The majority closes it.

**If these safeguards already exist, codify them. If they do not, this committee must.** **The principle is one sentence long: the decision to use lethal force must remain under meaningful human control.** [PAUSE]

Morocco has chosen. Who is with us?

## POI DEFENSE (post-speech fire)

**Q1. "Why name Belarus, the DPRK and Russia by name — is that not an attack?"**
Naming a public vote is not an attack; it is the record. The General Assembly's voting record is not an accusation — it is a document. If a delegation finds its own vote embarrassing to hear aloud, the problem is not my microphone.

**Q2. "Morocco claims twenty-nine — bloc arithmetic on the floor is campaigning, not debate."**
The working paper before this committee has twenty-nine sponsors from five continents; my sentence described the text, not a membership drive. Every delegation is free to verify the sponsorship list — that is rather the point of publishing it.

**Q3. "The US doctrine you quote is exactly the flexibility states need — why freeze one model?"**
Because "appropriate" is doing all the work in that sentence, and no international instrument defines it. A domestic dial each state sets for itself is not a floor — it is a ceiling nobody else can see. We are not freezing one model; we are pouring the foundation every model stands on.

**Q4. "You say twelve years, no names — the GGE was mandated progressively; your count is theatrical."**
Count from the first dedicated CCW meeting in 2014 and it is twelve; from the Human Rights Council debate, thirteen. By any count the result is identical: no instrument, no standards, no published determinations. The delegate is welcome to argue for a smaller number of empty years.

**Q5. "What gives Morocco — a drone buyer — standing to lecture this room on regulation?"**
Operator standing. We field these systems under human command and accept, in our own clauses, review, intervention capacity and recording for exactly the systems we fly. The states with only theory in their arsenals propose fear. Operators propose rules that survive contact.

**Q6. "So Morocco opposes all autonomous weapons?"**
Read the tiers. Systems a human cannot control, systems that kill humans without a human decision, systems no one can predict — prohibited. Everything else: permitted, under binding conditions. We oppose no technology. We oppose the absence of a name accountable for it.

**ALGERIA DAGGER:** *"Every clause Morocco sponsors binds Morocco — we wrote no fence around ourselves."* One sentence, no bilateral road trip, sit.
