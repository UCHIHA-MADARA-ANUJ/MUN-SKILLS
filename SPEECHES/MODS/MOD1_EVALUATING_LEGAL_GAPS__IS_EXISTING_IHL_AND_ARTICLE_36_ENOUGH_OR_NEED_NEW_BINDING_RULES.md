## THE SPEECH

<!-- SPEECH-START -->
Strip the "sufficient" camp down to its shield: Article 36 — **one paragraph**. It demands a review. **It does not say how. It does not say by whom. It does not say shown to whom.** The Secretary-General's report records it: states called reviews insufficient on their own.

**One hundred seventy-five states are bound to review new weapons. Twelve to fifteen are known to do it. Zero are required to publish.** Several states building these weapons never signed the Protocol — they grade their own exams anyway. **Nobody hands in the paper.**

Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates — two corrections, already in the text. **Shared criteria for every review, re-run after every modification — because an updated weapon is an unreviewed weapon. Recorded engagement data for every use of force — because a review nobody saw cannot answer for a strike everybody saw.** [PAUSE]
<!-- SPEECH-END -->
Article 36 says: trust yourselves. Binding rules say: show your work. **The law exists. The proof does not.**

*(Final three sentences slow and level. Sit mid-silence. No thank-you.)*

## POI DEFENSE — loaded for the counter-charge

**Q1. "The review duty is customary international law — it binds everyone already."**
Perhaps the duty is customary. The method never was. The ICRC's own guide records that Article 36 does not specify the manner or the authority of a review — so we hold a customary obligation wrapped in customary silence. What I proposed adds no new duty. It gives the existing duty a yardstick and a witness. If the duty is real, the delegate should welcome both.

**Q2. "Non-parties review weapons anyway — your 'gap' is imaginary."**
Then we agree on the fact and divide on its meaning. Several builders review without the Protocol — under national rules nobody else may read, to standards nobody else has seen. That is not a regime; that is simultaneity. Universality of the check. Absence of the yardstick. My speech named the missing piece precisely: shared criteria, shown results.

**Q3. "You said 'several builders never signed' — selective. Russia signed. Iran signed."**
I said several, and I chose the word carefully — the United States, Israel, Türkiye, India, Pakistan, China: unsigned. But party status was never my tripwire. Publication was. So let me widen the question to every delegation in this room, parties included: **name one state that has published the review outcome of a specific autonomous weapon.** Seventeen states describe how they would do it. The determinations themselves: none. I yield the floor to any delegation holding one.

**Q4. "Recording engagement data is a national-security non-starter."**
A record's existence and a record's publicity are two separate decisions — classification regimes exist precisely to hold sensitive records securely. Our clause demands the first; it leaves the second to the deploying state. No record at all is not security-consciousness. It is accountability-free operation by design — and no delegate here would accept that standard from their neighbour's army.

**Q5. "Is Morocco itself an AP I party with a functioning review? Sit down until you answer."**
This floor does not rise or fall on one delegation's filing cabinet — it rises or falls on whether any of us can verify any of us tonight. Every tripwire Morocco has proposed binds Morocco first; clause three contains no exemption for its author. When the questioning delegation tables its own published determinations, ours will not be the empty hands in the room.

**Q6. "Calling Article 36 insufficient is an attack on humanitarian law itself."**
The opposite. Article 36 is the floor this delegation stands on — we cite it, quote it, and build upward from it. What is on trial is not humanitarian law; it is the word "sufficient." A duty no one must demonstrate is honoured in statements and neglected in silence. We defend the law by making it checkable. That is the entire motion.

We wrote a fence around the trigger, not around any delegation. Happy to be tested against it."* One line. Do not get dragged bilateral. Sit.

## PROOF MANIFEST — every load-bearing claim, live-checked 8 Aug 2026

1. **"One paragraph… does not say how / by whom / shown to whom"** — Article 36 AP I verbatim (single paragraph): *"In the study, development, acquisition or adoption of a new weapon, means or method of warfare, a High Contracting Party is under an obligation to determine whether its employment would, in some or all circumstances, be prohibited…"* No method, no authority, no publication. → https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/article-36 · ICRC *Guide to the Legal Review of New Weapons* (2006): *"Article 36 does not specify in what manner and under what authority reviews… are to be constituted."* → https://www.icrc.org/sites/default/files/external/doc/en/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0902.pdf
2. **"175 bound / 12–15 known / zero must publish"** — Leupold Legal compendium (Mar 2026, collating ICRC data): *"As of February 2026, 175 States have ratified or acceded to Additional Protocol I… only 17 States have provided publicly available information on how they conduct weapon reviews."* → https://leupoldlegal.com/weapon-review-article-36-ap-i/ · SIPRI Insights 1501: *"of the 174 states parties… only a limited number of states (12 to 15) are known to have a weapon review mechanism in place;"* reviews *"beyond any kind of international oversight… no established standards."* → https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/files/insight/SIPRIInsight1501.pdf · Publication: A/79/88 ¶38 *"no requirement to publicize the outcome of such reviews."* → https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n24/154/32/pdf/n2415432.pdf
3. **"States themselves called reviews insufficient on their own"** — A/79/88 **¶37**: *"The view was expressed that legal reviews were insufficient on their own to address the concerns raised by lethal autonomous weapons systems, and that specific rules were required."* ⚠️ Attribute to **states in the SG's report**, never to the SG personally.
4. **"Several builders never signed"** — Non-parties to AP I per the ICRC live list include the United States, Israel, Türkiye, India, Pakistan, China. → https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/api-1977/state-parties · ⚠️ Say "February 2026 count" for 175; the list moves.
5. **"Grade their own exams anyway"** — US review practice without AP I: US Air Force Instruction quoted in the ICRC Guide (JAG to *"ensure all weapons… are reviewed for legality under international law prior to use in a conflict"*) — source 1, second link. POI line: documented for the US; the structural point covers every non-party reviewer.
6. **"An updated weapon is an unreviewed weapon"** — re-review of modifications exists only as good practice + the GGE's non-binding *"should"* (rolling text §IV.2, live-checked) — no binding instrument requires it. → https://reachingcriticalwill.org/images/documents/Disarmament-fora/ccw/2026/gge/documents/rolling-text-18dec25.pdf
7. **Clock insert ammo — "reviews can stop at a trade secret"** — GGE rolling text §IV.1: voluntary exchange of practices *"bearing in mind national security considerations or commercial restrictions on proprietary information."* Same PDF.
8. **Q3 trap precision** — "17 states describe how they would do it" = Leupold's "publicly available information on **how** they conduct weapon reviews" (source 2). Process papers ≠ weapon-specific determinations; no state publishes outcomes. Hold that distinction and the trap is unbreakable.

**Floor rule:** numbers stated as *"twelve to fifteen are known"* and *"one hundred seventy-five as of February 2026."* Precision is armor — someone in that room knows these sources.
