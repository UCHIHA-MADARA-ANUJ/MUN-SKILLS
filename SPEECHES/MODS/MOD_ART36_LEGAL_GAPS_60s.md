## THE SPEECH

<!-- SPEECH-START -->
Delegations say existing law suffices. They mean Article 36. Let us hold it to its own standard.

**One hundred seventy-five states are bound to review new weapons. Twelve to fifteen are known to do it. None are required to publish the result.** It does not even say how a review is conducted — the Secretary-General's own report records: reviews alone are insufficient.

Understand what "sufficient" means: a state tests its own weapon, by its own method, in private — and marks its own homework. That is not a safeguard. **It is a signature.**

Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates — two corrections, already in the text. **First: legal reviews against shared international criteria, before deployment and after any modification — because self-assessment is not oversight. Second: recorded engagement data on every use of force — because compliance that cannot be reconstructed cannot be believed.**

Article 36 says: check yourself. A binding rule says: show your work. [PAUSE]
<!-- SPEECH-END -->
**The law exists. The proof does not.**

*(Final line delivered cold, then sit. No thank-you.)*

## POI DEFENSE — expect fire after this one

**Q1. "The review obligation is customary — it binds even non-parties, so the law already covers everyone."**
Perhaps the duty is customary. The method never was. Even the ICRC's own guide says Article 36 does not specify how a review is conducted — so we hold a duty with no standard, no criteria, no publication. Customary obligation, customary silence. What I proposed adds no duty — it gives the existing duty a measurable shape.

**Q2. "You cite the Secretary-General's report as if it endorses your position. It records views — including that IHL suffices."**
Exactly — that is intellectual honesty, and I invite the same. The report records both camps, and then records the facts behind mine: no standard governs reviews, nothing must be published, and states themselves called reviews insufficient on their own. My speech quoted the record, not a preference.

**Q3. "Why would any major producer accept shared international criteria on its arsenal?"**
Because several of them already run domestic review directives at home — they wrote their own criteria and still operate. Codifying internationally the floor a state claims to keep costs that state nothing… unless the floor moves when nobody watches. Shared criteria are not a ceiling on power. They are a receipt that the floor exists.

**Q4. "Recording every engagement is a national-security non-starter."**
Our clause demands that a record *exist* — how a state classifies and guards it is that state's business. A record is not a broadcast. But an engagement with no record is not security-conscious; it is accountability-free by design. If your review process is real, it already generates exactly this paperwork.

**Q5. "Sit down, Morocco — is your delegation even an AP I party with a functioning review?"**
This floor does not need my government's paperwork to test a principle. Everything Morocco proposes binds Morocco — clause three contains no exemption for its author. When the delegation asking the question is ready to place its own reviews under shared criteria, we will be first in line beside them.

**Q6. "Isn't this Ireland's territory? They own Article 36 in this paper."**
Same wall, two bricks — ask Ireland. Their clauses strengthen the review itself; our clauses define what the review is measured against and who ever gets to check it. Standard plus structure. If the honourable delegate sees a duplication, it is because the wall finally has both.

## PROOF MANIFEST — every load-bearing claim (live links)

1. **175 states bound / 12–15 known to review / no standards, no oversight** — SIPRI Insights 1501 (2015): *"of the 174 states parties to Additional Protocol I, only a limited number of states (12 to 15) are known to have a weapon review mechanism in place;"* reviews are *"national procedures beyond any kind of international oversight"* with *"no established standards."* → https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/files/insight/SIPRIInsight1501.pdf
2. **175 parties as of Feb 2026; only 17 publish how they review** — Leupold Legal compendium (Mar 2026, collating ICRC data): *"As of February 2026, 175 States have ratified or acceded to Additional Protocol I. However, only 17 States have provided publicly available information on how they conduct weapon reviews."* → https://leupoldlegal.com/weapon-review-article-36-ap-i/
3. **"Article 36 does not specify how…"** — ICRC, *A Guide to the Legal Review of New Weapons* (2006), verbatim. → https://www.icrc.org/sites/default/files/external/doc/en/assets/files/other/icrc_002_0902.pdf
4. **"Reviews alone are insufficient" + "no provision governing how reviews should be conducted and no requirement to publicize the outcome"** — SG Report **A/79/88, ¶37–38**. ⚠️ Precision note: the report *records this as a view expressed by States* — say "the Secretary-General's own report records", never "the Secretary-General concluded". → https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n24/154/32/pdf/n2415432.pdf
5. **"Check yourself vs show your work"** — rhetorical framing of Art. 36's self-review design (ICRC guide: internal procedures only). Framing is ours; the design fact is source 3.

**Floor rule:** state numbers as "twelve to fifteen are known" and "seventeen publish how" — never inflate; the low numbers are already the scandal.
