## WHERE THE FIGHT ACTUALLY IS (bottom line, 3 sentences)

The world has spent 12 years "discussing" LAWS while the technology went operational on at least three battlefields. The majority (152 → 166 → 156 votes) wants law; a **tiny consensus-armed minority** (Russia, US, India, Israel, China-adjacent pragmatists, Korea, Australia) keeps the CCW from converting discussion into negotiation. **November 2026 is the cliff edge**: the GGE mandate dies at the 7th Review Conference, so either states convert the rolling text into a negotiation mandate, or a decade of work evaporates — and our committee exists to build the political pressure that decides it.

## WHAT THE LANDSCAPE TELLS US STRATEGICALLY `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`

1. **The centre of gravity moved.** The debate is no longer "should there be rules" — it's "who gets to block the negotiation launch." Frame all four mods as *input to the November decision*.
2. **The rolling text is the Schelling point.** Every mod topic maps to a rolling-text box (Mod 1 → Box I; Mod 2 → Boxes II–III; Mod 3 → Boxes III–IV; Mod 4 → Box V + gaps). Morocco's genius move: present our bloc's work as *completing* the rolling text, not competing with it.
3. **The UNGA track is the credible threat.** 96 states in a room in May 2025; 156 votes in parallel. If CCW fails in November, UNGA inherits the file. Say it plainly: *consensus is a working method, not a veto over humanity.*
4. **African momentum is fresh.** "A group of African states" joined the negotiations call in March 2026 — Morocco rides that wave as African co-convener.
