## B. WHAT THE GGE ROLLING TEXT CURRENTLY CONTAINS (the de facto draft) `[VERIFIED FACT unless noted]`

Structure (5 boxes, per Chair's summaries 2025–26, dig.watch process page, Stop Killer Robots March 2026 paper):

- **Box I — Working characterization.** 5 Mar 2026 formulation: *"a functionally integrated combination of one or more weapons and technological components, that can identify, select, and engage a target, without intervention by a human operator in the execution of these tasks."*
  - Clarifiers: human setting target parameters does NOT exclude the system; use against objects (non-lethal outcomes) does NOT exclude it.
  - Live fights: the word "lethal" (lethality is an effect, not a characteristic — ICRC objection); "identify+select" vs "select" alone; whether dispersed systems (sensor → cloud → weapon) count.
- **Box II — IHL application.** IHL applies fully — without reinterpreting existing obligations; link between IHL compliance and human control/judgement.
- **Box III — Prohibitions/restrictions.** Two-tier: systems that cannot be used in compliance with IHL → prohibited; others → regulated via "context-appropriate human judgement and control"; contested sub-elements: limits on number of engagements (6D(ii) — some call it IHL-incompatible), military objectives "by nature" (6D(iv) — some call it narrower than IHL), limits on real-time machine learning (6D(v)).
- **Box IV — Risk mitigation / lifecycle obligations.** Legal reviews, testing & evaluation, bias mitigation; Article 36-type content lives here.
- **Box V — Accountability & responsibility.** *"IHL imposes obligations on States, parties to armed conflict and individuals, not on machines. States, parties to armed conflict, and individuals must at all times remain responsible and accountable…"*

**Known gaps (attack them in Mods 2–4):** no anti-personnel prohibition in the text (Article 36 NGO + Stop Killer Robots + ICRC flag this); no IHRL engagement; weak "understandability of system & effects" requirement; definition may exclude dispersed/cloud-linked AI decision-support (Lavender-type) systems.

## C. THE IHL RULEBOOK THAT MUST BE APPLIED (Mod-2 ammunition)

The classic triad every judge knows:
- **Distinction (AP I Art. 48, 51, 52):** can a sensor + classifier distinguish combatants from civilians, and — harder — active combatants from *hors de combat* (wounded, surrendering)?
- **Proportionality (AP I Art. 51(5)(b), 57):** requires contextualized, value-laden judgement about expected civilian harm vs. concrete military advantage — the element least reducible to code.
- **Precautions (AP I Art. 57):** feasible precautions in attack, target verification, choice of means — who takes "precautions" inside an autonomous kill-chain?

Plus **Art. 35**: the right to choose methods of warfare is *not unlimited*; **Art. 36**: parties must review new weapons for legality; **Martens Clause** (AP I preamble): where no treaty text covers a case, humanity and public conscience constrain — **the ethics hook for anti-personnel bans**.

## D. WHAT THE SG REPORT A/79/88 PUT ON THE RECORD (quotable) `[VERIFIED FACT]`

- States identified applicable law: UN Charter, customary law, IHL, IHRL, international criminal law, **law of state responsibility**.
- "There was **no international legal instrument specifically regulating or prohibiting** lethal autonomous weapons systems."
- Humans retain responsibility; "accountability could not be transferred" to machines.
- Reasons states want an LBI (¶69): codify/progressively develop IHL; clarify IHL application to LAWS; **prevent regulatory fragmentation**; address humanitarian + ethical concerns; address **targeting of humans**; facilitate **equal participation of all states**.
- ¶75–76: many states want prohibition of systems operating completely outside human control and systems that can't comply with IHL; characteristics rendering systems non-compliant: inherently indiscriminate; unable to distinguish combatants/civilians.
- SG (via Stop Killer Robots summary + UNODA): "the autonomous targeting of humans by machines is **a moral line that must not be crossed**"; "time is running out."

**Floor-kill line:** *"Ninety-one states wrote to the Secretary-General. The record shows no devoted legal instrument, a majority demanding one, and the Secretary-General naming the moral line. Anyone claiming 'existing law suffices' must explain why the states bound by that law keep saying it does not."*

## E. MANDATE CARD — what a GA-style committee CAN and CANNOT do

| CAN | CANNOT |
|---|---|
| Recommend negotiation of a convention; create expert/Í working groups; request SG studies; adopt declarations; endorse draft articles | Bind states; create treaty obligations; amend CCW; compel the CCW consensus |
| Put the LAWS item on GA agenda permanently; mandate open-ended working groups (like the 2021–2025 OEWG precedents in ICT/cyber) | Force Russia/US/China to ratify anything |

**Morocco's play `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`:** draft a resolution that (1) welcomes the rolling text, (2) calls on the Nov 2026 RevCon to adopt a **negotiation mandate**, (3) requests the SG to **prepare the negotiating architecture** (draft rules of procedure, possible elements, venue options) so no month is lost, and (4) schedules a UNGA stock-take if the RevCon fails. This is maximum legal reach without overreach.
