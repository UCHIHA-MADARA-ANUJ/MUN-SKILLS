# 10 — ATTACK PREDICTION & DEFENSE MATRIX (MOROCCO)
**File:** Attack Prediction Engine + Cross-Examination Drill Coach output
**Ranking:** likelihood × damage, highest first. Every defense = admit what's true → reframe → evidence → redirect. 20-second drills included.

---

## ATTACK 1 — "Morocco operates loitering munitions (Harop) with autonomous terminal homing. You're a drone power preaching arms control." ⚠️⚠️⚠️ *Certain. High damage if fumbled.*

**Facts we own:** `[VERIFIED]` 13 TB2s ($70M, 2021); reported Harop purchase; 100+ BlueBird ISTAR drones; berm ISR role.

**Defense (say it):**
> *"The delegate reads our record correctly — and draws the wrong conclusion. Morocco operates unmanned systems **under human command**: pilots, two-way datalinks, human authorization of force. Nothing in our position bans remotely-piloted aircraft or human-supervised munitions. We target one thing: weapons that select and engage **without human intervention**. The state writing rules for knives may own a kitchen."*

**Follow-up kill:** *"Who better to draft these rules — states that fly these systems daily and know where human control genuinely lives, or states guessing from a podium?"*

## ATTACK 2 — "Algeria says Moroccan drones killed civilians (Nov 2021 truck incident). Clean your own house."

⚠️⚠️ *Likely if Algeria is in room. High emotional charge.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"Morocco does not litigate contested allegations in this committee — the claim the delegate cites was never independently verified, and the delegate knows that. What Morocco WILL say is this: precisely because modern drone warfare can produce contested incidents, we need binding rules on transparency, logging, and accountability — the very mechanisms this bloc is tabling. If the delegate's government supports verifiable accountability, welcome to the working paper."*

**Drill discipline:** never repeat the allegation's details; never say "civilian truckers"; label `[CONTESTED CLAIM]`, bridge to mechanism, close. 15 seconds max.

---

## ATTACK 3 — "Morocco signed the US Political Declaration — soft law. Now you demand treaties. Which is it — are declarations enough or not?" ⚠️⚠️⚠️ *Certain from pragmatists. Moderate damage.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"Both — in sequence. The Declaration gathers practice; binding law disciplines it. Morocco endorsed the Declaration in November 2023 and voted for every General Assembly resolution on this file since. Our position has been a straight line for thirteen years — since May 2013. The states with a consistency problem are those who sign declarations promising 'appropriate human judgment' and then refuse to define 'appropriate' in law. Perhaps the delegate can name one?"*

*(Notice: converts their attack into THEIR hypocrisy exposure. Do not gloat; offer the drafting seat at the end.)*

---

## ATTACK 4 — "Morocco buys Israeli tech and follows Western positions. You're not a neutral African voice." ⚠️⚠️ *Possible from NAM hardliners/Iran orbit. Low-moderate damage.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"Morocco's position was formed in 2013 — years before any of the partnerships the delegate references — and it sits inside the African Group's common line: meaningful human control, always. We co-sponsor with Brazil, Sri Lanka, Cuba, and Kenya. If that is a Western position, then the Global South has stolen it. Morocco's passport to this debate is written in African consensus, not procurement contracts."*

---

## ATTACK 5 — "Your two-tier 'prohibition+regulation' is a license to keep YOUR drones while banning others'." *Moderate likelihood. Moderate damage.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"Read the tiers: they regulate **functions**, not flags. If a Moroccan system selects and engages persons without human intervention, the treaty binds Morocco exactly as it binds every delegations in this room — with logs, reviews, and limits. That is the difference between law and favouritism. Will the delegate make the same pledge for their systems? Because we just did, publicly."*

---

## ATTACK 6 — "You're splitting consensus by threatening a UNGA track. Why undermine the CCW?" *High likelihood from consensus-orthodox states. Moderate damage.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"Morocco defends the CCW — we cite its rolling text in every speech. What we cannot defend is a working method converted into a veto over humanity, forever. The General Assembly is not a bypass; it is the parliament of states, and it has spoken 152, then 166, then 156 strong. We are not threatening the CCW; we are reminding it of its deadline — which, honourable delegates, expires in November."*

---

## ATTACK 7 — "Marrakesh doesn't have a national Article 36 review process. Physician, heal thyself." *Low likelihood (obscure jab) but brutal if unready.*

**Defense (say it):**
> *"The delegate makes our argument better than we could. Most developing states lack the infrastructure for Article 36 reviews — which is exactly why Morocco's package includes shared review capacity, methodology exchange, and a support fund. We do not pretend perfection; we legislate so no state needs to pretend. Join us in funding it — or explain why only wealthy militaries deserve legal review."*

## THE THREE-SECOND BRIDGES (when cornered, say one, then move)

- *"Contested claim, chair — the record shows otherwise; our mechanism makes records verifiable for all."*
- *"If the delegate's standard is consistency, let us measure both our records by the same treaty they refuse to sign."*
- *"Morocco's doctrine is thirteen years old — the delegate's objection is thirteen seconds old."* (use sparingly — judges like ONE sting, not a swarm)

## DELIVERY DOCTRINE

1. Answer the **harshest honest part first** (yes, we fly drones) → credibility skyrockets.
2. 20 seconds max per defense; then bridge to mechanism.
3. End every defense with a door held open: *"…and there is a seat for your delegation on that clause."* Judges score bridge-builders.
